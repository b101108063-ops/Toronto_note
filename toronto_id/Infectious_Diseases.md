Bacteria Basics
+ bacteria are prokaryotic cells that divide asexually by binary fission
+ Gram stain divides most bacteria into two groups based on their cell wall
= GP: thick, rigid layer of peptidoglycan
«= GN: thin peptidoglycan layer + outer membrane composed of lipoproteins and lipopolysaccharides
= clinical significance: Gram stain results are used to guide tailored empiric treatment prior to availability
of culture and susceptibility testing results
+ acid-fast bacilli (AFB): high mycolic acid content in cell wall (e.g. Mycobacterium species)
= called “acid-fast” because they resist stain decolourization despite acid-alcohol washes
+ partially AFB: bacteria with moderate mycolic acid content that will decolourize with the acid-alcohol used
in AFB stains but are considered partially acid-fast positive based on a modified acid-fast stain with a weaker
acid during the washout phase (e.g. Nocardia)
= note that Nocardia species will be acid-fast stain negative and modified acid-fast stain positive, while
Mycobacterium species will be positive on both acid-fast and modified acid-fast stains
+ atypical bacteria: not seen on Gram stain and difficult to culture
= obligate intracellular bacteria (e.g. Chlamydia): cannot live outside host cell
bacteria lacking a cell wall (e.g. Mycoplasma)
spirochetes (e.g. Treponema pallidum)
Oz can be either vital or detrimental to growth
obligate aerobes: require O>
obligate anaerobes: require environment without O:
facultative anaerobes: can survive in environments with or without 02

Mechanisms of Bacterial Disease
+ 1. adherence to and colonization of skin or mucous membranes
= fimbriae (pili): microfilaments extending through the cell walll attach to epithelial cells (e.g. E. coli in the
urinary tract)
2. invasion or crossing epithelial barriers
3. evasion of host defense system through:
= inhibition of phagocytic uptake via polysaccharide capsule (e.g. Streptococcus pneumoniae, Neisseria
meningitidis, Haemophilus influenzae)
= presence of surface proteins (e.g. Staphylococcus, Streptococcus)
4. toxin production
= exotoxins are secreted by living pathogenic bacteria and cause disease even if the bacteria are not present
(eg. Clostridium tetani, Clostridium botulinum)
= endotoxins are structural components of GN bacterial cell walls and may be shed by live cells or released
during cell lysis
5. intracellular growth
= obligate intracellular (e.g. Rickettsia, Chlamydia)
= facultative intracellular (e.g. Salmonella, Neisseria, Brucella, Mycobacteria, Listeria, Legionella)
6. biofilm
= an extracellular polysaccharide network forming mesh around the bacteria (e.g. Staphylococcus
epidermidis) which can coat prosthetic devices such as IV catheters

Virology

iral Basics
+ viruses are infectious particles consisting of RNA or DNA covered by a protein coat
« infect cells and use host’s metabolic machinery to replicate
= nucleic acid can be double stranded (ds) or single stranded (ss)
= nucleic acid can be positive sense (+) or negative sense (-)
= canbe enveloped or naked -
+ virions: mature virus particles that can be released into the extracellular environment Helical
+ host susceptibility is governed by the host cell, virus surface proteins (viral tropism), and cellular immunity

Viral Disease Patterns
1, acute infections (e.g. adenovirus)
= host cells are lysed in the process of virion release
= some produce acute infections with late sequelae (e.g. measles virus-induced subacute sclerosing
panencephalitis (SSPE, also known as Dawson's Disease))
2. chronic infections (>6 mo) (e.g. HBV, HIV)
= host cell machinery is used to produce and chronically release virions
3. latent infections
« viral genome remains latent in host cell nucleus
= can reactivate (e.g. HSV, VZV)

= overgrowth of normal flora (e.g. Candida spp.)

Parasitology

Parasite Basics
+ parasite: an organism that lives in or on another organism (host) and damages the host in the process
+ parasites with complex life cycles require more than one host to reproduce
= reservoir host: maintains a parasite and may be the source for human infection
= intermediate host: maintains the asexual stage of a parasite or allows development of the parasite to
proceed through the larval stages
= definitive host: allows the parasite to develop to the adult stage where reproduction occurs
+ 3 major groups of parasites: protozoa, helminths, and ectoparasites; some arthropods are also parasitic
+ see Table 26, ID33 and Table 27, 1D39 for examples of clinically important parasites

Mechanisms of Parasitic Disease
1. mechanical obstruction (e.g. ascariasis, clonorchiasis)
2. competition with host for resources (e.g. anemia in hookworm disease, vitamin B12 deficiency in

. cytotoxicity leading to abscesses and ulcers (e.g. amoebiasis, leishmaniasis)

= autoimmune (e.g. myocarditis of Chagas disease, tissue destruction of mucocutaneous leishmaniasis)

+ can be caused by routine infections that are common in non-travellers (e.g. URTI, UTI)

History
«+ pre-travel preparation

«= dates of travel (determine incubation period)

= season of travel: wet or dry

«= destination: country, region (urban or rural), environment (jungle, desert, etc.)

= purpose of trip

= style of travel: lodgings, camping, adventure travelling

= transportation: use of animals

= local population: sick contacts

= More likely to travel for a longer duration and stay in local homes consuming local food and water
exposure history

= street foods, untreated water: increased risk of traveller's diarrhea, enteric fever

= uncooked meat/unpasteurized dairy: increased risk of parasitic infection

= body fluids (sexual contacts, tattoos, piercings, IVDU, other injections)

= animal/insect bites: increased risk of malaria, dengue, rickettsioses, rabies
fever pattern
incubation period: use the earliest and latest possible dates of exposure to narrow the differential diagnosis

= CXR

+ empiric treatment if ongoing fever for 48-72 h and negative malaria smears and all cultures pending
+ travelled to India, Southeast Asia: azithromycin + doxycycline
