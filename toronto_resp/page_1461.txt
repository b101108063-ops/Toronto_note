Asthma

«+ see Family Medicine, FM19 and Pediatrics, P91

Definition

+ chronic inflammatory disorder of the airways resulting in episodes of reversible bronchospasm causing
airflow obstruction

+ associated with reversible airflow limitation and airway hyper-responsiveness to endogenous or exogenous
stimuli

+ inflamed airways undergo a variety of changes including hypertrophy of airway smooth muscle and
hyperplasia of mucous-producing goblet cells

Epidemiology
common, 10.8% of Canadians (3.8 million); 8-10% of adults, 10-15% of children (however often
misdiagnosed due to inaccurate clinical diagnosis, failure to use objective testing)

+ often family history of atopy (asthma, allergic rhinitis, eczema)

+ work-related asthma (includes work-exacerbated asthma, or occupational asthma caused by high or low
molecular weight sensitizer exposure)

Pathophysiology

+ Severe asthma attack:

+ airway obstruction > V/Q mismatch > hypoxemia > tventilation > +PaCO2 > tpH and muscle fatigue >
ventilation, # PaCOs/+pH

Signs and Symptoms

+ dyspnea, wheezing, chest tightness, cough, sputum

symptoms usually occur or worsen at night or early morning

symptoms can be paroxysmal or persistent

when having an asthma attack: signs of respiratory distress, pulsus paradoxus

Treatment
+ confirma diagnosis
+ environment and prevention: identify and avoid triggers, influenza and pneumococcal vaccine
+ patient education: features of the disease, goals of treatment, self-management asthma action plan, inhaler
technique
+ pharmacological
= symptomatic relief in acute episodes: short-acting B2-agonist or combined, long acting B2-agonist with
inhaled corticosteroid (formoterol/budesonide)
