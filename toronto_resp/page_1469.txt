Unknown Etiologic Agents
IDIOPATHIC PULMONARY FIBROSIS

Definition

+ pulmonary fibrosis of unknown cause with UIP histology (found on biopsy or inferred from CT)
+ aprogressive, irreversible condition

+ DDx: connective tissue disease associated-ILD, chronic HP, asbestosis, NSIP

Signs and Symptoms
+ commonly presents over age 50, incidence rises with age; males > females; smoking is a risk factor
+ dyspnea on exertion, nonproductive cough, late inspiratory fine crackles at lung bases, clubbing

Investigations
+ labs (nonspecific; autoimmune serology usually negative)

+ CXR: reticular or reticulonodular pattern with lower lung predominance; often see honeycombing in
advanced disease

high resolution CT: typical pattern is one of UIP; ground glass, consolidation, or nodules should not be
prominent in IPF

+ biopsy: only if patient has an indeterminate for UIP or alternative diagnosis to IPF pattern on CT imaging

Treatment
+ acute exacerbation:

= prednisone - minimal evidence but frequently used

= antibiotics and diuretics are considered on an individualized basis + mechanical ventilation
+ ongoing management:

= antifibrotic therapy: pirfenidone or nintedanib

= smoking cessation and pulmonary rehab + O2

= PPL if patient has reflux

= lung transplantation for advanced disease

= palliative care

CRYPTOGENIC ORGANIZING PNEUMONIA and ORGANIZING PNEUMONIA

Definition

* organizing pneumonia: an inflammatory process that involves the bronchioles and alveolar space, caused
by connective tissue disease associated ILD, medications, or other triggers

* cryptogenic organizing pneumonia: organizing pneumonia without an identifiable cause

Signs and Symptoms

+ nonspecific cough, dyspnea, recurrent pneumonia, fever, and fatigue; may be associated with an acute flu-
like illness

symptoms present for <2 mo in most patients

OP can be misdiagnosed as community acquired pneumonia, but a lack of response to empiric antibiotics
suggests OP

+ COP: no history of connective tissue disease

Investigations

+ CXR: ground-glass opacities and patchy consolidation - usually bilateral, but can be unilateral, and can be
migratory on serial X-rays

high resolution CT: air space consolidation in patchy distribution is most common, but can be more

nodular or ground glass density - “reverse halo” is a classic sign involving consolidation surrounding areas

of ground glass

+ labs: ANA, RF, creatine kinase, antitopoisomerase, anticentromere antibody, anti-double-stranded DNA,
ANCA, myositis antibodies - assess for rheumatologic cause

+ bronchoscopy with lavage to exclude infections or malignancy
surgical lung biopsy or transbronchial cryobiopsy

diagnosed based on consistent imaging in the correct clinical context

Treatment

+ COP: Immunosuppression with steroids - often recurrent and may require long taper with steroids,
typically for 6 mo

+ other forms of OP: treatment can be longer or shorter than 6 mo (e.g. drug induced OP may improve with
removal of the drug alone)
