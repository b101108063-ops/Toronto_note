Chronic Venous Insufficiency

Definition

+ wide spectrum of chronic venous disease with advancing symptoms of edema, skin changes, varicosities, or

leg ulcers

Epidemiology

+ primary venous insufficiency (99% of CVI cases)is the most common venous disorder of the lower
extremities

+ 65% of North American adult population develops some degree of venous insufficiency

Etiology
+ spectrum of chronic venous disease involving deep and superficial lower extremity veins caused by calf

muscle pump dysfunction, venous obstruction, and chronic valvular incompetence (reflux) due to phlebitis,

varicosities, or DVT
primary CVI: due to venous valve incompetence or obstruction. Suspected risk factors: age, hormonal
contraceptives, prolonged standing, pregnancy, obesity

secondary CVI: caused by DVT, pelvic tumors, congenital anomalies, AV fistulae, trauma, and rarely,
pregnanc,

+ both lead to venous hypertension and inflammatory changes

Clinical Features and Complications
leg swelling (non-specific): ankle and calf edema; relieved by foot elevation (CEAP C3)
pain (most common) :fullness/tightness/aching, worse at day's end
veins: (see Table 4, CEAP Classification of Venous Disease, VS11)
telangiectasias: small, dilated intradermal veins (<3 mm diameter). Spider web veins are a subtype
reticular: small, dilated subdermal veins (<3 mm diameter)
varicose: large, dilated, and tortuous superficial veins (>3 mm diameter), often from the great or small
saphenous veins and tributaries, caused by incompetent valves in the deep, superficial, or perforator
systems (CEAP C2)
ulceration: usually located above the medial malleolus. Characteristics: shallow, wet/weeping, irregular
edges and without significant pain
