G36 General and Thoracic Surgery
Investigations
+ laborato:

. mild leukocytosis with left shift (may have normal WBC counts)
«= higher leukocyte count with perforation
. BCG to rule out ectopic pregnancy
= urinalysis
+ imaging
= US: may visualize appendix, but also helps rule out gynecological causes - overall accuracy 90-94%,
can rule in but CANNOT rule out appendicitis (if >6 mm, sensitivity, specificity, positive predictive
value, negative predictive value 98%). Less reliable in patients with igh BMI and retrocecal appendix
position, and may not reliably identify the presence of a phlegmon
= CT scan: thick wall, enlarged (>6 mm), wall enhancement, appendicolith, and inflammatory changes -
overall accuracy 94-100%, optimal investigation
= MRI may be useful to diagnose appendicitis in pregnancy if ultrasound is non-diagnostic

Treatment
+ hydrate, correct electrolyte abnormalities
+ for non-perforated:
= appendectomy is the gold standard, laparoscopic is standard
= complications: intra-abdominal abscess, appendiceal stump leak
= perioperative antibiotics: cefazolin + metronidazole; if uncomplicated, perioperative dose is adequate
= randomized data supports that antibiotics alone is also a safe and effective way to treat non-perforated
appendicitis if there is no appendicolith (success rate 80% to >90%). If successfully managed, the risk
of recurrent appendicitis within 1 year is ~15%. Success rates are worse with an appendicolith and non-
operative management is not recommended
= no specific indication for colonoscopy in this setting, other than general colon cancer screening
+ for perforated (usually associated with longer 4-5 d history):
# “ifabscess, consider radiologic drainage and antibiotics for 14 d with possible interval appendectomy
once inflammation has resolved
« if phlegmon but no abscess, antibiotics only (since there is nothing to drain)
* occasionally, can consider upfront surgery but usually surgery is reserved for failure of nonoperative
management. Consider treatment with postoperative antibiotics
= colonoscopy can be considered especially if >50 yr to rule out concurrent etiology (neoplasm)

Prognosis
* mortality rate: 0.09-0.24%

Inflammatory Bowel Disease

* see Gastroenterology, G22

Principles of Surgical Management
+ lifestyle and medical management remain first line. Surgery can alleviate symptoms, address complications,
and improve quality of life when symptoms are too severe or failed to respond to initial treatment
+ perioperative management
= optimize medical status: may require TPN (especially if >7 d NPO) and bowel rest
= perioperative stress dose of steroids may be required depending on dose and duration of steroid use
. slucocorticoids are aknown risk factor for 30 d postoperative infections and should be weaned to a
aily dose < 20mg if possible. Conversely, immunomodulators can typically be safely continued in the
perioperative period
» VTE prophylaxis: LMWH or heparin (IBD patients at increased risk of thromboembolic events)

Crohn’s Disease

« see Gastroenterology, G23

Treatment
+ surgery is for symptom management; it is NOT curative, but over lifetime ~70% of Crohn’s patients will
have surgery
+ indications for surgical management
« failure of medical management
= SBO (due to stricture/inflammation): indication in 50% of surgical cases
= abscess, fistula (enterocolic, vesicular, vaginal, cutaneous abscess), quality of life, perforation,
hemorrhage, chronic disability, failure to thrive (children), and perianal disease
+ surgical procedures
= resection and anastomosis/stoma if active or subacute inflammation, perforation, or fistula
«= endoscopic dilation and stricturoplasty for small bowel and colorectal strictures
= stricturoplasty — widens lumen in chronically scarred bowel: relieves obstruction without resecting
bowel (contraindicated in acute inflammation)
= surgery should be attempted in the elective setting ideally off steroids
+ resection margin only has to be free of gross disease (microscopic disease irrelevant to prognosis)

Complications of Treatment
+ anastomotic leak
+ dehydration
