Pathogenesis

+ risk factors
= diet (low-fibre, high fat, red meat), inactivity, elevated BMI, older age, and smoking
= muscle wall weakness from aging and illness (e.g. Ehlers-Danlos, Marfan’s)
+ high intraluminal pressures cause outpouching to occur at points of greatest weakness, most commonly
where vasa recta penetrate the circular muscle layer leading to an increased risk of hemorrhage

Clinical Features

+ uncomplicated diverticulosis: asymptomatic (70-80%)

episodic abdominal pain (often LLQ), bloating, flatulence, constipation, diarrhea

absence of fever/leukocytosis

no physical exam findings or poorly localized LLQ tenderness

complications:
= diverticulitis (15-25%): 25% of which are complicated (i.e. abscess, obstruction, perforation, fistula)
= bleeding (5-15%): PAINLESS rectal bleeding, 30-50% of massive LGIB
= diverticular colitis (rare): diarrhea, hematochezia, tenesmus, and abdominal pain

Treatment
+ uncomplicated diverticulosis: high fibre, education
+ diverticular bleed
= initially workup and treat as any LGIB
= if hemorrhage does not stop, resect involved region

Diverticulitis

Epidemiology
+ 95% left-sided in patients of Western countries, 75% right-sided in Asian populations

Pathogenesis

erosion of the wall by increased intraluminal pressure or inspissated food particles > inflammation and
focal necrosis > micro or macroscopic perforation

usually mild inflammation with perforation walled off by pericolic fat and mesentery; abscess, fistula, or
obstruction can ensue

poor containment results in free perforation and peritonitis
