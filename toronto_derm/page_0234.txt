Hypertrichosis

Clinical Features

+ excessive hair growth that is an outlier for age, sex, or race

+ is not associated with androgen sensitive areas

+ can be localized/circumscribed, or generalized, acquired or congenital

Etiology
+ congenital causes of hypertrichosis:
« localized: congenital circumscribed hypertrichosis: congenital melanocytic nevus , congenital Becker
nevus, smooth muscle hamartoma, underlying neurofibroma
= generalized: congenital hypertrichosis lanuginosa, congenital generalized hypertrichosis, Brachmann-
de-Lange syndrome, teratogen exposure
= acquired causes of hypertrichosis:
= localized: acquired Becker nevus, chemicals (.e. iodine, minoxidil, psoralens, TCS), orthopedic casts/
splits, fracture, friction (ie. scratching in lichen simplex) , trichomegaly (long eyelashes that can arise
rom local latanoprost treatment)
= generalized: cerebral disturbances, acrodynia, infection, malnutrition, dermatomyositis, thyroid
abnormalities

Management
+ acquired: treat the underlying cause
+ congenital: hair removal (i.e. shaving, waxing, laser hair removal), makeup, lighten hair with commercial

bleach or 6% hydrogen peroxide
