Management
+ patient education is crucial - especially that atopic dermatitis is a chronic disease with periods of flares
and periods of remission
counsel atopic dermatitis flares are rarely related to foods
stress reduction if indicated as it can trigger flares
use only fragrance-free products since it isa common irritant and/or allergen
be vigilant for depressive symptoms and the possible need for psychiatric referral, especially among those
with severe disease
consider early referral to dermatology for patients with severe dermatitis who may require advanced
therapy like biologics
non-pharmacologic therapy
= moisturizers
apply liberally at least daily to entire body (e.g. Eucerin’ Eczema Cream, CeraVe* Cream, Cetaphil* Cream)
= use only fragrance-free non-soap cleansers (e.g. Eucerin® Eczema Body Wash, Cetaphil" gentle
cleanser, Dove’ Sensitive Skin Bar Soap)
pharmacologic therapy
= topical corticosteroids
effective in reducing acute flares
choice of steroid potency depends on age, body site, duration use
apply one adult fingertip unit (0.5 g) to an area the size of two adult palms BID for at least 2 wk for acute
ares
most common local side effects: skin atrophy, purpura, telangiectasia, striae, and acneiform eruption
rare local side effects: hypopigmentation {more commonly seen in patients of colour, especially with higher
potency TCS and prolonged use, without breaks)
