ANAGEN EFFLUVIUM

Clinical Features
+ hairloss due to insult of hair follicle impairing its mitotic activity (growth stage)

Pathophysiology

« precipitated by chemotherapeutic agents (most common), other medications (bismuth, levodopa,
colchicine, cyclosporine), exposure to chemicals (thallium, boron, arsenic)

+ dose-dependent effect

+ hairloss 7-14 d after single pulse of chemotherapy; most clinically apparent after 1-2 mo

+ reversible effect; follicles resume normal mitotic activity few wk after agent stopped

ALOPECIA AREATA

Clinical Features

+ autoimmune disorder characterized by patches of complete hair loss often localized to scalp, but can affect

eyebrows, beard, eyelashes, etc.

may be associated with dystrophic nail changes — fine stippling, pitting

“exclamation mark” pattern (hairs fractured and have tapered shafts, i.e. looks like “!”)

may be associated with autoimmune conditions: pernicious anemia, vitiligo, thyroid disease, Addison’s
isease

spontaneous regrowth may occur within mo of first attack (worse prognosis if young at age of onset and

extensive loss)

+ frequent recurrence often precipitated by emotional distress

Management

excellent prognosis for localized disease

TCS and intralesional steroids (triamcinolone acetonide) can be used for isolated patches
topical immunotherapy (diphencyprone, anthralin and squaric acid dibutyl ester)
systemic immunosuppressants for refractory or extensive disease

newer treatments: topical and oral JAK inhibitors

+ irreversible loss of hair follicles with fibrosis

Etiology
+ physical: radiation, burns
+ infections: fungal, bacterial, TB, leprosy, viral (HSV)
+ part of the follicular occlusion tetrad (along with HS, acne conglobata, and pilonidal disease)
+ primary inflammatory - subdivided into lymphocytic, neutrophilic, and mixed
