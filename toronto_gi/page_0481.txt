Primary Biliary Cholangitis (formerly Primary Bi

Definition
+ chronic inflammation and fibrous obliteration of intrahepatic bile ductules

iology/Epidemiology
likely autoimmune (associated with Sjégren’s syndrome, scleroderma, CREST syndrome (calcinosis,
Raynaud’s phenomenon, esophageal dysfunction, sclerodactyly, telangiectasia), RA, thyroiditis)

affects mainly middle-aged women (M:F=1:

Signs and Symptoms

+ often asymptomatic

+ initial symptoms: pruritus, fatigue

+ chronic: jaundice melanosis (darkening skin), xanthelasma (white or yellow bumps under the skin around
the eyes) and other signs of cholestasis

end-stage: hepatocellular failure, portal HTN, ascites

high incidence of osteoporosis

Investigations

+ increased ALP, GGT; bilirubin rises in later stage

positive anti-mitochondrial antibodies (AMA; 95% specificity and sensitivity)

elevated IgM often seen (but not universal)

increased serum cholesterol (mild increase in LDL, larger increase in HDL)

= may have: xanthelasmas, xanthomas

«+ liver biopsy confirms diagnosis and stage severity but is usually not required; elevated ALP for >6 mo and
positive AMA is sufficient for diagnosis

+ normal bile duct on MRCP rules out large/medium duct disease as cause for elevated ALP
“overlap” syndromes with autoimmune cholangitis, autoimmune hepatitis and rarely sclerosing cholangitis

are possible, but very rare

Treatment
+ drugs that treat the underlying disease:
= ursodeoxycholic acid (UDCA ; first line treatment)
= obeticholic acid (if inadequate response to ursodiol)
cholestyramine (for pruritus and hypercholesterolemia)
calcium and vitamin D for low bone density; consider bisphosphonates if osteoporosis
repeated monitoring of liver enzymes and LFTs (q 3-6 mo), TSH (annually), and BMD (q 2-4 yr, pending
baseline BMD)
ensure vaccines are updated (notably HAV & HBV); avoid EtOH, smoking
liver transplant if disease severe, progressive

Prognosis

+ canbe fatal, although not all asymptomatic patients show progression

+ treatment improves prognosis - response to treatment with UDCA (normalization or near-normalization of
ALP) is associated with improved survival comparable to age- and sex-matched controls
