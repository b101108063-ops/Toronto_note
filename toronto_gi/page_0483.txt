Pathophysiology

+ activation of proteolytic enzymes within pancreatic cells, starting with trypsin, leading to local and
systemic inflammatory response

+ in gallstone pancreatitis, this is due to mechanical obstruction of the pancreatic duct by stones

+ inethanol-related pancreatitis, pathogenesis is unknown

+ inrare genetic diseases, mutations prevent the physiological breakdown of trypsin required normally
to stop proteolysis (e.g. mutant trypsin in hereditary pancreatitis or mutation in SPINK1 gene, which
normally inhibits activated trypsin); may be model for ethanol-related pancreatitis

Investigations

increased serum pancreatic enzymes: amylase, lipase (more specific)
ALT >150 specific for biliary cause

increased WBC, glucose, low calcium

imaging: CT most useful for diagnosis and prognosis

= x-ray: “sentinel loop” (dilated proximal jejunum), calcification, and “colon cut-off sign” (colonic spasm)

= U/S: useful for evaluating biliary tree (67% sensitivity, 100% specificity)

» CT scan with IV contrast: most useful when done >I d after presentation, helpful for diagnosis and
prognosis because contrast seen only in viable pancreatic tissue, non-viable areas (as necrotic areas
cannot take up contrast dye) can be biopsied percutaneously to differentiate sterile from infected
necrosis ... numerous scales to describe severity: probably most useful is proportion of pancreas not
taking up contrast on CT done 48 h after presentation

+ ERCP or MRCP if cause uncertain, assess for duct stone, pancreatic or ampullary tumour, pancreas
divisum

+ ofnote, MRCP is usually conducted to determine the etiology while ERCP is reserved for those patients who
require biliary intervention to treat (i.e. stone, cancer)

Classification
+ interstitial edematous vs. necrotizing
+ mild, moderate, severe

Prognosis
+ usually a benign, self-limiting course, single or recurrent
+ occasionally severe leading to:
= shock
pulmonary edema
multi-organ dysfunction syndrome
Gl ulceration due to stress
death
* numerous scales to describe severity: probably most useful is proportion of pancreas not taking up
contrast on CT done 48 h after presentation (necrotic pancreas does not take up the contrast dye)
* presence of organ failure (especially for >48 h), is associated with worse outcomes
