Irritable Bowel Syndrome

Definition
+ a disorder of gut brain interaction characterized by the presence of abdominal pain and alteration in bowel
habit

Epidemiology

+ 5-7% worldwide prevalence

+ onset of symptoms usually in young adulthood
+ FoM

Clinical Features
+ Rome IV Criteria are used for diagnosis
+ diagnosis is based chiefly on history; no specific diagnostic test available

Pathophysiology
multifactorial in nature and can vary between patients
disorder of the gut-brain axis
hallmark in most patients is the presence of visceral hypersensitivity
patients often have alterations in central processing of pain signals coming from the gut
increasing evidence of immune cell dysfunction, changes in intestinal permeability, changes in gut
serotonin signaling and/or alterations in the gut microbiome in some patients with IBS
altered GI motility can be seen in a subset of patients with IBS but not all patients with IBS
psychological factors can contribute to the development and severity of IBS. Presence of early life
trauma/adversity, a diagnosis of anxiety/depression, exposure to chronic stressors are risk factors for the
development of IBS
4 subtypes of IBS that are determined based on the patient’s predominant stool form using the Bristol Stool
Chart
«= IBS-D: IBS with predominant diarrhea
«= IBS-C: IBS with predominant constipation
«= IBS-M: IBS-mixed with both diarrhea and constipation (each >25% of all abnormal bowel movements)
«= IBS untyped: insufficient abnormalities to be IBS-C, D, or M
