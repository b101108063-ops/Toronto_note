Ways to Estimate GFR Using Serum Creatinine Concentration
1. estimate GFR using CKD-EPI equation
«= the current most accurate equation
= calculated using serum Cr, age, biological sex, and race
= equation performance improves at low GFR
= use an online medical calculator to perform this calculation, given formula complexity
2. measure CrCl - 24h urine collection
« calculation provides reasonable estimate of GFR
= GER/d = (urine [Cr] x 24h urine volume)/(plasma [Cr])
= must use same units for urine [Cr] and plasma [Cr]
3. estimate CrCl using the Cockcroft-Gault equation
= CrCl = 1.2 * (140 - age) x weight (kg)/(Cr] (for women, multiply this term by 0.85)
= Thisis mainly used in clinical medicine to help with dosage of renally excreted medications in patients
with CKD (e.g. many antibiotics)
4, estimate GFR with new Cr and cystatin C-based equations
= serum Cr and cystatin C values used with age and sex to estimate GFR (excludes the race variable)
= more accurate than CKD-EPI with race omitted

Limitations of Using Serum Cr Measurements
1, must be in steady state
= constant GFR and rate of production of Cr from muscles
= sudden kidney injury (i.e. AKI) may reduce GFR substantially, however, serum Cr will not immediately
reflect sudden reduction in GFR until new Cr steady state is reached: the increase in serum Cr will lag
behind fall in GFR for a few days
2. GFR must fall substantially before plasma [Cr] rises above normal laboratory range due to compensatory
‘actors
= with progressive renal failure, remaining nephrons compensate with vascular autoregulation and
hyperfiltration
= GFR is relatively preserved despite significant structural damage
3. plasma [Cr] is influenced by the rate of Cr production
= lower production with smaller muscle mass (e.g. female, elderly, low weight)
e.g. consider plasma [Cr] of 100 pmol/L in both of these patients
— 20 yr lean man who weighs 100 kg, GFR = 144 mL/min
— 80 yr woman who weighs 50 kg, GFR = 30.6 mL/min
= clinical correlation: GER decreases with age, but this is not reflected asa rise in serum Cr due to the
age-associated decline in muscle mass
4, tubular secretion of Cr increases as GFR decreases
= calculations of eGER using serum Cr overestimate GFR
= certain drugs (cimetidine, trimethoprim) interfere with Cr secretion (causing serum Cr to rise without
a fall in GFR)
5. errors in Cr measurement (in older lab evaluation methods)
= very high bilirubin level causes (Cr] to be falsely low
= acetoacetate (a ketone body) and certain drugs (cefoxitin) create falsely high (Cr]

Measurement of Urea Concentration

+ urea is the major end-product of protein metabolism

+ urea production reflects dietary intake of protein and catabolic rate; increased protein intake or catabolism
(sepsis, trauma, GI bleed) causes increase in urea level

a significant amount of urea is reabsorbed along the tubule (~50% with normal ECF volume, but this can
rise significantly with ECF volume depletion)

therefore, ECF volume depletion causes a greater proportional rise in urea level than in serum [Cr]

typical ratio of urea to [Cr] in serum is 1:12 in SI units (using mmol/L for urea and umol/L for Cr)
