Complications of Acute Hyponatremia

+ cerebral edema and increased intracranial pressure resulting in nausea, headaches, decreased LOC,
seizures, coma, brain herniation, and death

+ risk of brain cell shrinkage with rapid correction of hyponatremia

Complications of Chronic Hyponatremia

+ can develop osmotic demyelination of pontine and extrapontine neurons; may be irreversible (e.g. central
pontine myelinolysis)

+ symptom onset may be delayed 2-6 d; begins as dysarthria, dysphagia, paresis, movement disorders > later
on seizures, lethargy, confusion, disorientation, obtundation, coma

Risk Factors for Osmotic Demyelination
«+ low serum [Na*] of <120 mmol/L at presentation and/or duration of hyponatremia >2 d
«= especially likely to occur if very low serum [Na*] at presentation (<106 mmol/L)
+ associated hypokalemia, malnutrition, liver disease, alcoholism
+ overly rapid correction of [Na’], ie. rise in [Na’] >8 mmol/L/24 h if chronic hyponatremia
= inappropriate sodium replacement
« restoration of euvolemia leading suppression of ADH by restoring euvolemia with isotonic fluid
= stopping a reversible stimulus of SIADH (e.g. replace corticosteroid in a patient with Addison disease,
stop causative medications e.g. SSRIs)
= discontinuation of thiazides; depriving water in patient with psychogenic polydipsia

Investigations for Hyponatremia

ECF volume status assessment (see Table 4, NP9)

serum electrolytes, glucose, Cr

serum osmolality, urine osmolality

urine Na* (urine Na* <10-20 mmol/L suggests volume depletion as the cause of hyponatremia)

assess for causes of SIADH (see Table 5, NP11)

TSH, free T4, and cortisol levels

consider CXR and possibly CT chest if suspect pulmonary cause of SIADH (e.g. paraneoplastic syndrome by
small cell lung cancer)

consider CT head if suspect CNS cause of SIADH (e.g. subarachnoid hemorrhage)

Treatment of Hyponatremia
+ general measures for all patients
1, restrict free water intake (<500-1500 ml/d depending on severity of hyponatremia)
. treat underlying cause (e.g. restore ECF volume if volume depleted, remove offending drug,
treat pain, nausea, etc.) but do so very carefully, given risk of overly rapid correction of chronic
hyponatremia
if euvolemic or hypervolemic hyponatremia, promote free water loss - furosemide or urea tablets
carefully monitor serum Na’, urine volume, and urine osmolality
frequent monitoring for rapid overcorrection - brisk urine output (free water diuresis), frequent
urine and serum electrolytes
* monitor urine output frequently: high output of dilute urine is the first sign of dangerously rapid correction
of hyponatremia as the stimulus for ADH is diminished with the correction of hypovolemia

A. Known Acute (known to have developed over <48 h)
+ commonly occurs in hospital (dilute IV fluid, postoperative increased ADH)
+ less risk from rapid correction since adaptation has not fully occurred
+ if symptomatic

= correct rapidly with 3% NaCl; use boluses of 100-150 cc (or 2 cc/kg), aiming for rapid increase (within 1
h) of 4-6 mmol/L; this should lead to resolution of symptoms in most patients
continue 3% NaCl at ~1 mmol/L/h to increase serum [Na*] to ~130 mmol/L (but limit rise to 12
mmol/L/24h if duration is > 24 h)
if asymptomatic, treatment depends on whether patient is at risk for further fall in serum [Na*], and/or
already has high ICP
if patient is at risk of further fall in serum [Na*] and/or already has raised ICP, then should aim to raise
serum [Na‘] by ~5 mmol/L over ~5 hr using 3% NaCl at ~1 mL/kg/h; can then increase more slowly to
~130 mmol/L, but limit rise to no more than 12 mmol/L/24h if duration is >24h
+ if asymptomatic, treatment depends on presence of risk factors for osmotic demyelination syndrome (ODS)

and volume status (see next section)

goal is to promote a negative balance of free water
restrict free water intake
promote free water loss by administration of furosemide with salt tablets, oral urea, or vasopressin
receptor 2 antagonists (e.g. tolvaptan)
if ODS risk factors present, aim to correct serum sodium more slowly
do not give 3% NaCl if hyponatremia is autocorrecting due to water diuresis
B. Chronic
1. if severe symptoms (seizures or decreased LOC)

= must partially correct acutely, using same acute strategy as for acute and symptomatic as described

above
= limit total rise to 8 mmol/L in 24h
= may need furosemide
