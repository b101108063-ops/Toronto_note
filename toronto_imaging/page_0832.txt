NPI2 Nephrology

Signs and Symptoms
+ hypernatremia = shrunken cells

= acute hypernatremia (<48 h)

= chronic hypernatremia (>48 h), cells will have achieved adaptive mechanism: can import and generate
new osmotically active particles to normalize cell size
most cases of hypernatremia will be due to chronic hypernatremia
acute hypernatremia primarily presents in patients with diabetes insipidus, post obstructive diuresis,
and post ATN diuresis (without free water access); occasionally from administration of hypertonic IV
fluids (e.g. hypertonic NaHCOs during cardiac arrest resuscitation), or ingestion of seawater
symptoms due to brain cell shrinkage: altered mental status, weakness, neuromuscular irritability,
focal neurologic deficits, seizures, coma, death
+ + polyuria, thirst, signs of hypovolemia

Complications
+ can develop intracranial hemorrhage as brain pulls away rapidly from skull
+ can develop osmotic demyelination from rapid rise in serum [Na’]

+ overly rapid correction of chronic hyponatremia may lead to cerebral edema

Treatment of Hypernatremi:
+ general measures for all patients
= give free water (oral or IV)

= treat underlying cause

= monitor serum Na’ frequently (q4h) to ensure correction is not occurring too rapidly
if evidence of hemodynamic instability, then must first correct volume depletion with NS bolus
loss of free water (which caused the hyponatremia) is often accompanied by loss of Na’, but a
proportionately larger water loss
+ encourage patient to drink water, as PO is preferred for fluid administration
+ if unable to replace PO or NG, correct H20 deficit with hypotonic IV solution (IV DSW, 0.45% NS [half

normal saline], or 3.3% dextrose with 0.3% NaCl (“2/3 and 1/3”])

+ acute hypernatremia: use formula to calculate water deficit. Replace entire water deficit within 24 h (hourly
infusion rate = water deficit in mL/24h)
infusion rate may need to be increased in order to account for ongoing losses in addition to initial deficit
chronic hypernatremia: aim to lower serum sodium by 8-10 mEq/L in 24 h (often achieved by giving free
water at 1.35 mL/kg/h)

Diabetes Insipidus

Definition
+ collecting tubule is impermeable to water due to absence of ADH or impaired response to ADH
+ defect in central release of ADH (central DI) or renal response to ADH (nephrogenic DI)

Etiology
+ central DI: neurosurgery, granulomatous diseases, trauma, vascular events, and malignancy
+ nephrogenic DI
= usually acquired - drugs (e.g. lithium), secondary to amyloidosis, sickle cell disease, Sjogren syndrome,
polycystic kidney disease, electrolyte imbalances (hypercalcemia, hypokalemia)
congenital/hereditary
post ATN diuresis
post obstructive diuresis

Diagnosis
+ urine osmolality inappropriately low in patient with hypernatremia (Uss= <300 mOsm/kg)
+ serum vasopressin concentration may be absent/low (central), or elevated (nephrogenic)
+ can also measure plasma copeptin (copeptin is secreted from hypothalamus along with vasopressin, but is
more stable and easier to measure)
+ dehydration test: test used if the patient is not hypernatremic when being assessed
= patient is deprived of water under close supervision, to cause hypernatremia, and the test is continued
until one of the following develops:
concentrated urine (Uosm > 300 mOsm/kg);
concentrated plasma (serum [Na*] > 146), or
loss of 3% or more in body weight
if urine osmolality remains <300 (fails to concentrate urine), most likely DI
administer DDAVP (exogenous form of ADH) 2 mcg IV
if Uosm rises >300, then they have central DI
if Uosm does not rise, then they have nephrogenic DI

Management
+ central DI: administer exogenous ADH (e.g. DDAVP) 10 ug intranasally or 2 wg SC or IV
+ nephrogenic DI: patients may have partial or complete ADH resistance, and DDAVP is generally ineffective
= maintain fluid intake to match losses, e.g. PO water, IV D5W, IV 0.45% NS
«= treat underlying cause of nephrogenic DI
