Hyponatremia

Definition
+ serum [Na*] <135 mmol/L

+ can be associated with hypo-osmolality (most common), iso-osmolality, or hyperosmolality
+ consider if it is associated with “appropriate” (hypovolemia) vs. “inappropriate” (euvolemia) ADH secretion
+ ifappropriate ADH secretion, is it real vs. effective volume loss?

Signs and Symptoms

+ depend on degree of hyponatremia and, more importantly, rate of progression from onset

hyponatremia = swollen cells

acute hyponatremia (<48 h) more likely to be symptomatic
chronic hyponatremia (>48 h) less likely to be symptomatic due to cellular adaptation
= adaptation: normalization of brain volume through loss of intracellular electrolytes (within hours) and

organic osmolytes (within days)

seizures, decreased LOC, brain herniation, and death

neurologic symptoms predominate (secondary to cerebral edema): headache, nausea, malaise, lethargy,
weakness, muscle cramps, anorexia, somnolence, disorientation, personality changes, depressed reflexes,
