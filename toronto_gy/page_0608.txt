Classification
+ International Federation of Gynecology and Obstetrics (FIGO) classifies fibroids by location and type

= submucosal: grow within myometrium, can grow into endometrial cavity (FIGO type 0 - pedunculated,
type 1 - <50% intramural, type 2 - >50% intramural)
intramural: most common, grow within the muscular wall of the uterus (FIGO type 3 - contacts
endometrium and is 100% intramural, type 4 - intramural)
subserosal: grow from the serosa (FIGO type 5 - > 50% intramural, type 6 - <50% intramural, type 7 -
pedunculated)
cervical: located in cervix (FIGO type 8 - other)

Investigations

+ bimanual exam: uterus asymmetrically enlarged

+ pelvic and/or transvaginal U/S: to confirm diagnosis and assess location of fibroids

+ sonohysterogram: useful for differentiating polyps from submucosal fibroids, assessing size and degree
of submucosal component, or assessing presence of submucosal fibroids which may impact fertility, or for

planning hysteroscopic resection

diagnostic office hysteroscopy: can be used instead of a sonohysterogram in patients with suspected

submucosal fibroids or those desiring fertility to visualize the endometrial cavity, helps with planning of

hysteroscopic submucosal fibroid resection, and rules out small polyps not visualized on U/S

occasionally MRI is used for preoperative planning (e.g. before consideration of laparoscopic myomectomy)

endometrial biopsy: used in patients with AUB to rule out endometrial hyperplasia or cancer (especially i

age >40 yr or risk factors (type 2 DM, obesity, chronic anovulation))

CBC: anemia (only found if associated with AUB/heavy menstrual bleeding)
