Treatment

. surgical confirmation of disease is NOT required prior to starting medical management. Asymptomatic
endometriosis does not require treatment. Management depends on certainty of the diagnosis, severity
of symptoms, extent of disease, desire for future fertility, and impact to GI/GU systems (e.g. intestinal
obstruction)

+ medical

= medical suppression to prevent progression of disease, unless trying to conceive, which has not been
roven in the literature
«= NSAIDs (e.g. naproxen sodium - Anaprox*). Avoid selective COX-2 inhibitors (celecoxib, rofecoxib,
valdecoxib) in those who are attempting conception as some studies indicate these drugs can prevent or
delay ovulation
= Istline
+ cyclic/continuous estrogen-progestin (OCP)
* progestin (IM medroxyprogesterone (Depo-Provera’) or oral dienogest (Visanne"))
+ Mirena’ IUS
= 2nd line
* GnRH agonist (e.g, leuprolide (Lupron’) or elagolix (Orlissa*)): suppresses pituitary gland and induces
pseudomenopauses. Not a contraceptive
— side effects: hot flashes, vaginal dryness, reduced libido
— Lupron use >6 mo: include add-back progestin or estrogen to prevent decreased BMD, reduce
vasomotor side effects
— Orlissa’ low dose (150 mg once daily) does not require add-back progestin or estrogen. High
dose (200 mg PO BID) does require add-back
+ Myfembree® (40mg ralugolix, estradiol Img and norethidrone acetate 0.5 mg) isa GnRH antagonist
used to treat moderate to severe pain associated with endometriosis, and with add back included to
counter vasomotor side effects
¢ danazol (Danocrine’): weak androgen
— side effects: weight gain, fluid retention, acne, hirsutism, voice change (not commonly used
due to side effects)
+ surgical
= limited to failure of medical treatment, or desire for definitive treatment
= conservative laparoscopy using laser, electrocautery + laparotomy
+ ablation/resection of implants, lysis of adhesions, ovarian cystectomy of endometriomas
= definitive: hysterectomy + bilateral salpingo-oophorectomy
= best time to become pregnant is immediately after conservative surgery
= if patient is not planning to become pregnant postoperatively, suppress ovulation medically to prevent
recurrence (not proven but recommended)

+ above treatments are for the pain, not for the infertility associated with endometriosis, which usually
involves surgery + assisted reproductive technologies. Also, management for endometriomas is surgical for
symptomatic and expanding masses, but this can decrease ovarian reserve, so if it is asymptomatic and small
(<5 cm), then no surgery is necessary

 ifconsidering surgery for endometriomas, particularly if bilateral, refer to REI for consideration of
ovarian freezing

|Adenomyosis CO

Definition
+ extension of areas of endometrial glands and stroma into the myometrium
+ synonym: “endometriosis interna” (uterine wall may be diffusely involved)

Epidemiology

+ 15% of females >35 y/o; found in 20-40% of hysterectomy specimens

+ mean age at presentation: 40-50 y/o (older age group than seen in endometriosis)
+ adenomyosis is a common histologic finding in asymptomatic patients

Clinical Features

+ often asymptomatic
+ heavy menstrual bleeding, secondary dysmenorrhea, pelvic discomfort
+ dyspareunia, dyschezia

+ uterus symmetrically bulky, usually <14.cm

+ Halban’s sign: tender, softened uterus on premenstrual bimanual exam

= danazol 100-200 mg PO once daily (trial x 4 mo)
+ surgical
= definitive: hysterectomy — treatment of choice in patients who have completed childbearing
