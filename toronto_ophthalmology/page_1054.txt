+ recurrent hordeola

+ conjunctivitis

+ keratitis (from poor tear film)

+ corneal ulceration and neovascularization

Treatment

+ warm compresses, lid massages, and lid washing using commercially available eyelid scrub solution
+ topical antibiotics, or oral antibiotics (doxycycline) for posterior blepharitis, as needed

« if severe, ophthalmologist may prescribe a short course of topical corticosteroids, omega-3 fatty acids

Xanthelasma

Definition
+ eyelid xanthoma (lipid deposits in dermis of lids)

Clinical Features

appear as pale, slightly elevated yellowish plaques or streaks
most commonly on the medial upper lids, often bilateral
associated with hyperlipidemia (~50% of patients)

common in the elderly, more concerning in young people

Treatment
+ excision for cosmesis only, commonly recurs

+ thin, vascular mucous membrane

+ three layers
« bulbar conjunctiva: lines sclera to limbus (junction between cornea and sclera)
= conjunctival fornices: superior, inferior, lateral, and medial regions
= palpebral (tarsal) conjunctiva: lines inner surface of eyelid

Pinguecula fo)

Definition
+ yellow-white subepithelial deposit of hyaline and elastic tissue adjacent to the nasal or temporal limbus,
sparing the cornea

Clinical Features

* associated with sun and wind exposure, aging-associated degeneration

+ benign, sometimes enlarges slow

+ may be irritating due to rapid breakup of the tear film formation over the pingueculae, as they are elevated
off the globe

Treatment

. Protect eyes from UV light, wind, dust

+ surgery for cosmesis and symptoms if severe

+ irritative symptoms can usually be adequately treated with lubricating drops

Pterygium a)

Definition
+ fibrovascular, triangular, wing-like encroachment of epithelial tissue onto the cornea
+ high dose of UV light destroys the limbal stem cells enabling the conjunctiva to invade the cornea

Clinical Features
+ eye irritation, lacrimation, foreign body sensation
+ may induce astigmatism, decrease vision

Treatment

+ excision for chronic inflammation, threat to visual axis, and/or cosmesis

irritative symptoms may be treated with lubricating drops

+ one-third recur after bare excision, lower recurrence with conjunctival autograft and transplant of limbal
stem cells (~5%)

Subconjunctival Hemorrhage a

blood beneath the conjunctiva, otherwise asymptomatic

idiopathic or associated with trauma, Valsalva maneuver, bleeding disorders, HTN, anticoagulation
give reassurance if no other ocular findings, resolves spontaneously n2-3.wk

360° involvement should be highly suspicious for globe rupture if trauma history

if recurrent, consider medical hematologic workup, especially if non-traumatic in nature
