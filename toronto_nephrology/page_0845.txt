Rapidly Progressive GN

+ asubset of nephritic syndrome in which the clinical course occurs over days to weeks

+ etiology can be divided into low and normal complement levels

+ frequently immune-mediated, with Ig and C3 deposits found in GBM; but may be pauci-immune and
caused by an ANCA vasculitis

nephritic syndromes usually denote proliferative glomerulonephritis

clinical and histopathological syndrome (e.g. glomerular crescents on biopsy)

any type of proliferative GN can present as RPGN

additional etiologies seen only as RPGN: anti-GBM disease and granulomatosis with polyangiitis (GPA)
(previously called Wegener's granulomatosis)

crescentic GN (glomerular crescents identified by pathology) is frequently seen in RPGN resulting from
proliferation of parietal epithelial cells and is the most aggressive form of glomerular disease

Treatment and Prognosis

treatment and prognosis depends on etiology

treatment: underlying cause if post-infectious; corticosteroids and cyclophosphamide or other cytotoxic
agent (azathioprine and cyclophosphamide, mycophenolate mofetil, and biologics such as rituximab), and
plasmapheresis to manage cases such as anti-GBM disease

pulse steroid therapy and other immunosuppression (steroid sparing agents), BP control (with RAAS
agents), plasma exchange, monitoring for progression to ESRD
