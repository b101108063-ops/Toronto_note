Lesion Localization

+ CNSvs. PNS lesion
= CNS: cortical, subcortical, brainstem (midbrain, pons, medulla), cerebellum, spinal cord
= PNS: anterior horn cell, nerve root, plexus, peripheral nerve, neuromuscular junction, muscle
= see Table 3, N4 for differentiating LMN, UMN, and extrapyramidal lesions
+ cortical
= contralateral hemiparesis (with differential effect on face and arm vs. leg)
= contralateral hemisensory loss, cortical sensory loss (graphesthesia, stereognosis)
= dominant hemisphere: aphasia, alexia, agraphia, acalculia, left-right disorientation
= non-dominant hemisphere: hemineglect, dysprosody, amusia, constructional apraxia, alien hand
syndrome
homonymous hemianopsia/quadrantanopsia
gaze deviation (involvement of frontal eye field)
seizure
agnosia (visual, auditory)
= ideomotor and ideational apraxia
+ subcortical
= internal capsule: contralateral hemiparesis with equal face, arm, and leg involvement without
sensory/cortical deficits (pure motor); contralateral hemiparesis and sensory deficit (sensorimotor);
contralateral dysmetria/clumsiness and paresis (ataxic hemiparesis); dysarthria and ataxia of the hand
(clumsy hand-dysarthria syndrome)
« basal ganglia: resting tremor, bradykinesia, festinating gait, hemiballism, chorea, dystonic posture
«= thalamus: dense sensory loss, contralateral severe pain, visual field defect, cognitive impairment,
altered LOC
+ brainstem (midbrain, pons, medulla)
= crossed hemiplegia or hemisensory loss (i.e. ipsilateral face, contralateral body)
= ipsilateral limb ataxia (dysmetria, impaired rapid alternating movements)
= nystagmus, diplopia, ptosis, pupil abnormalities (miosis, mydriasis), gaze impairment, internuclear
ophthalmoplegia
= dysphagia, dysarthria
= hearing loss, vertigo
= ipsilateral Horner's syndrome
+ cerebellum
= symptoms ipsilateral to side of lesion
= limb ataxia (dysmetria, intention tremor, dysdiadochokinesia, hypotonia causing pendular reflexes)
= truncal ataxia (wide-based gait, inability to perform heel-to-toe gait, truncal titubation)
= nystagmus, hypermetric or hypometric saccades, impaired smooth pursuit
= scanning speech (explosive speech with noticeable pauses and accentuated syllables)
+ spinal cord
= LMN signs at level of lesion; UMN signs below level of lesion
= sensory level (line below which there is sensory loss); suspended “cape-like” sensory level (in central
cord lesions)
= neurogenic bladder and bowel, sexual dysfunction
«= saddle anesthesia (involvement of conus medullaris)
= sensory ataxia (involvement of dorsal columns)
+ anterior horn cell
= weakness without sensory symptoms
= fasciculations, atrophy, cramps
= for example: ALS, spinal muscular atrophy, Kennedy disease, West-Nile virus, poliomyelitis
+ nerve root
= deficits beyond distribution of a single peripheral nerve
= myotomal/dermatomal deficits
= back/neck pain radiating to limb
= saddle anesthesia (involvement of cauda equina)
