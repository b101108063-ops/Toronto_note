Management

+ education: explanation of the mechanism of functional constipation for parents/older children

+ clean out (especially if there is encopresis): PEG 3350 (1-1.5 g/kg/d, often higher doses needed), picosalax,
PEGlyte® (inpatient care)

maintenance: adequate fluid intake (if <6 mo, 150 mL/kg/d), adequate dietary fibre (fruit, vegetables,
whole grains), medications (PEG 3350, Senna), appropriate toilet training technique (dedicated time for
defecation: 3-10 min, 1-2 x/d)

children should be treated for at least 3-6 mo, and should not be weaned from maintenance therapy until
they are having regular bowel movements without difficulty

+ regular follow-up with ongoing support and encouragement is essential

Complications
+ pain retention cycle: anal fissures and pain from withholding passing stool, chronic dilatation + overflow
incontinence

Organic Causes of Constipation

+ endocrine/metabolic: hypothyroidism, DM, hypercalcemia, CF, lead poisoning, hypokalemia

+ neurologic: spinal cord abnormalities/trauma, NF, Hirschsprung’s disease (see General and Thoracic
Surgery, GS74), cerebral palsy

+ anatomic: bowel obstruction, anorectal anomalies (imperforate, atresia, stenosis, anteriorly displaced)
drugs: lead, chemotherapy, opioids

other: celiac disease, generalized joint hypermobility

Acute Abdominal Pain

History

. descr ton of pan (location, radiation, duration, constant vs. colicky, relation to meals)

+ alleviating and aggravating factors: with movement, after a bowel movement, after vomiting, etc.
+ associated symptoms: N/V, diarrhea, fever

Physical Exam
+ abdominal exam, rectal exam (guided by history/physical, should be completed with a chaperone), rash

Investigations
+ may include CBC, differential, liver enzymes, lipase, bilirubin, creatinine, CRP, glucose, blood gas,
urinalysis to rule out UTI, B-HCG, imaging (abdominal, pelvic, and/or testicular)
