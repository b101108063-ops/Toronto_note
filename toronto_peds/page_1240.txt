Management

+ correct fluid and electrolyte losses (see Fluids and Electrolytes, P39)

+ administer appropriate nutrition, including zinc supplementation; consider vitamin A supplementation if
there are signs of deficiency

assess need for antibiotics

involve public health as needed

+ prevention: vaccination against measles, rotavirus and cholera, breastfeeding, good hygiene, optimal
nutritional status

Gastroenteritis

History
+ non-specific: diarrhea, vomiting, fever, anorexia, headache, myalgias, abdominal cramps

+ viral causes most common, bacterial and parasitic agents more common in older children (2-4 yr)
+ recent infectious contacts: symptoms usually begin 24-48 h after exposure

Physical Exam
+ febrile
+ dehydrated: must assess extent (see Approach to Infant/Child with Dehydration, P39)

Investigations

+ not usually necessary in young children

+ CBC, electrolytes, and stool studies may be indicated in severe cases, if IV hydration required or atypical
presentation

+ microbiologic testing is indicated for immunocompromised hosts and children with atypical features (e.g.
visible blood or mucus in stool, persistent diarrhea > 7 days, international travel, exposures to unsafe foods,
farm animals, and untreated water, etc.)

= viral: fecal viral assays
