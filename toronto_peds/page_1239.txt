Differential Diagnosis
+ Gl obstruction: pyloric stenosis, malrotation with volvulus, intussusception, Hirschsprung disease
+ other GI disorders: achalasia, gastroparesis, gastroenteritis, peptic ulcer, IBD

+ neurologic: hydrocephalus, subdural hematoma, intracranial hemorrhage or mass

+ metabolic/endocrine: galactosemia, hereditary fructose intolerance, urea cycle defects

+ toxic: lead poisoning

+ cardiac: heart failure, vascular ring, autonomic dysfunction

+ infectious: sepsis/ meningitis, UTI, upper and lower airway infections, otitis media, hepatitis

+ renal: obstructive uropathy, renal insufficiency

+ others: child neglect, self-induced vomiting

Investigations
+ thriving baby requires no investigation
+ GERD generally can be a clinical diagnosis, diagnostic investigations rarely done but may include:
= upper GI series — assesses anatomy and motility disorder
= esophageal pH — assesses frequency and duration of acid exposure, not a definitive diagnostic test and
not done often
= upper endoscopy and esophageal biopsy - rule out other conditions that mimic GERD symptoms (e.g.
eosinophilic esophagitis), assesses GERD-related esophageal injury
+ warning signs of associated disorders requiring further investigations: bilious vomiting, GI tract bleeding,
forceful vomiting, fever, lethargy, hepatosplenomegaly, bulging fontanelle, micro/macrocephaly, seizures,
abdominal tenderness/distension, suspected genetic, metabolic syndrome, or chronic disease

Complications
+ esophagitis, oral feeding aversion, poor weight gain, aspiration, strictures, Barrett's esophagus
