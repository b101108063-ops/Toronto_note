Hypoglycemia

Definition
+ glucose <2.6 mmol/L within 72 h of birth OR <3.3 mmol/L after first 72 h of life

Etiology

+ decreased carbohydrate stores: premature, SGA

+ endocrine: hormonal deficiencies (GH, cortisol, epinephrine), insulin excess (infant of maternal GDM,
LGA, Beckwith-Wiedemann syndrome/islet cell hyperplasia), hypothalamic-pituitary-adrenal axis

suppression (panhypopituitarism)

inborn errors of metabolism: fatty acid oxidation defects, galactosemia

miscellaneous: sepsis, hypothermia, polycythemia, perinatal stress (e.g. asphyxia), maternal labetolol use

(common with preeclampsia)

Clinical Findings
+ signs often non-specific and subtle: jitteriness, lethargy, poor feeding, irritability, tremors, apnea, cyanosis,
seizures

Management
+ identify and monitor infants at risk (pre-feed blood glucose checks) until blood glucose stable and for at
least 12 h (for infant of maternal GDM or LGA) or 24 h (if preterm or SGA)

in a well at-risk infant, begin oral feeds as soon as possible after birth and ensure regular feeds, check
glucose at 2h of age

if significant and/or symptomatic hypoglycemia, provide glucose IV (D10W) and titrate according to blood
sugar levels

if persistent hypoglycemia (>72 h of life and glucose <2.8 mmol/L), prolonged glucose IV, severe
symptomatic hypoglycemia (coma, lethargy, seizure), or no predisposing cause, send “critical blood work”
during an episode of hypoglycemia: confirmatory plasma glucose, ABG, ammonia, B-hydroxybutyrate,
cortisol, carnitine, acylcarnitine profiling, free fatty acids, GH, insulin, lactate, urine dipstick for ketones

Definition

Clinical Features
+ jaundice typically visible at serum bilirubin levels of 85-120 pmol/L
