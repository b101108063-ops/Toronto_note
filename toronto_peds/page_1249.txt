Immune Thrombocytopenic Purpura

Definition
+ ITPis isolated thrombocytopenia (platelet count <100 000/uL with normal WBC count and Hb)

Epidemiology

+ most common cause of symptomatic thrombocytopenia in childhood
+ peak age: 2-5 yr, M>F Glightly)

+ incidence 5 in 100 000 children per yr

Etiolog
. «autsed by autoantibodies that bind to platelet membranes > Fc-receptor mediated splenic uptake >
destruction of platelets

Clinical Features

+ 60% present within 1 mo after viral illness (e.g. URTI, chicken pox)

+ sudden onset of petechiae, purpura, bleeding in an otherwise well child

clinically significant bleed in only 3% (severe bleed more likely with platelet count <10) with <0.5% risk of
intracranial bleed

no lymphadenopathy, no hepatosplenomegaly

Investigations

. diagnosis made in well-appearing patients with mucocutaneous bleeding without other systemic symptoms
or signs and lab confirmation of ITP (diagnosis of exclusion)

+ labs: thrombocytopenia with normal Hb and WBC on CBC and blood smear

+ bone marrow aspirate only indicated if red flags on history, exam, or lab studies (not needed in initiation of
corticosteroid treatment or before splenectomy)

+ testing for antinuclear antibodies not needed in a typical case of presumed ITP

Differential Diagnosis
+ Destructive thrombocytopenias
= immune-mediated: immune thrombocytopenia, drug-induced thrombocytopenia, systemic
autolimiine disorders (e.g. SLE, autoimmune lymphoproliferative syndrome, autoimmune hemolytic
anemia, etc.
platelet activation and consumption: microangiopathic disorders (e.g. HUS, thrombotic
thrombocytopenic purpura, etc.)
mechanical destruction
sequestration and trapping: hypersplenism
decreased platelet production
infection: EBV, CMV, sepsis, etc.
nutritional deficiencies: folate, B12, iron
acquired bone marrow failure: aplastic anemia, medications, radiation
infiltrative bone marrow disease: leukemias, lymphomas, etc.
genetic causes of impaired thrombopoiesis

Management
+ involve family in management; shared decision-making

= aim to achieve a platelet count associated with adequate hemostasis to reduce the risk of serious

bleeding (i.e. > 10-20x 10°/L)
no or mild bleeding (i.e. only bruising/petechiae) - consider watchful waiting (irrespective of platelet levels)
moderate bleeding (i.e. severe skin manifestations with some mucosal lesions and some troublesome
epistaxis or menorrhagia) ~ IVIg (1 g/kg) or steroids; if Rh-positive or DAT-negative can use anti-D (not
indicated in low Hb from blood loss or autoimmune hemolysis)
severe bleeding (i.e. prolonged epistaxis, GI bleeding, or intracranial hemorrhage) - immediate treatment
with IV steroids and Vig: may use tranexamic acid as adjunct therapy
treatment with IVIg or prednisone if mucosal or internal bleeding, Platelets <10, or at risk of significant
bleeding (surgery, dental procedure, concomitant vasculitis, or coagulopathy)
IVIg = faster increase in platelet count
life-threatening bleed: additional platelet transfusion + emergency splenectomy
persistent (>3-12 mo) or chronic (>12 mo): re-evaluate; treat if symptoms persist
supportive: avoid contact sports and ASA/NSAIDs
