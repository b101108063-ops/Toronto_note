Immunization of Immunocompromised Patients
susceptibility to infection and vaccine response varies
individualized vaccine schedule based on patient’s immune status
inactivated vaccines: may be administered if indicated; responses may be reduced or absent and duration of
immunit may be reduced (compared to healthy individuals); dose increase or extra booster doses may be
indicate:
live attenuated vaccines:

= avoid if severely immunocompromised or if uncertain of immune status

= can be given to those with isolated IgA, IgG subclass or complement deficiencies, or asplenia

= live viral vaccines can be provided for most children with phagocyte or neutrophil disorders, but live

bacterial vaccines are contraindicated

additional vaccines: may need vaccines that are not usually recommended for otherwise healthy children or
not usually administered beyond a certain age
timing: vaccines should be administered when maximum immune response is expected (i.e. when not
immunosuppressed medically or pharmacologically)
response: as immune response may be inadequate, consider post-immunization antibody titres if
appropriate; positive serologic test may be due to immunoglobulin therapy or maternal antibody (infants
<18 mo)

Vaccine-Hesitant Parents
+ healthcare professionals’ vaccine advice plays a key role in parental decision-making

«= do not dismiss vaccine-hesitant families in your practice
use a presumptive approach (for giving the vaccine) and motivational interviewing

= use open-ended questions and listen to concerns and opinions - do not assume the health concerns of
the parent
address concerns non-judgmentally and non-confrontationally; validate why parents may hold their
belief
use compelling stories of vaccine-preventable disease
communicate clearly to discuss disease risks and vaccine benefits and risks
address immunization pain and discuss options for pain reduction (e.g. breastfeeding, rub/stroke the skin
near injection site, distraction techniques)
community protection (herd immunity)

= “wait and see” approach to vaccination in an outbreak scenario is not advisable
