Physical Exam
+ physical findings not explained by underlying medical condition
assess growth parameters and compare to past recorded parameters (weight, height, head circumference)
multiple injuries not explained by accidental injury or child’s development level
patterned skin injuries: linear, shapes, etc. that do not match provided history
injury location:
= bruises: on areas with abundant soft-tissue cushioning, such as the abdomen, buttocks, genitalia, fleshy
part of cheek or on ears, neck, or feet; bruises that do not fit described cause. Can use TEN-4-FACESp,
a dlinical decision making tool to screen for concerning bruising in children <4 yr (https://research.

research-and-evaluation-center/tricam/ten-4-facesp/)
« fractures: posterior ribjmetaphyseal/scapular/vertebral/sternal fractures
= immersion burns (e.g. hot water)
altered mental status: head injury, poisoning
eyes: retinal hemorrhages
scalp: patchy hair loss from traumatic alopecia or severe malnutrition
oral exam: check the frenula for tears
traumatic head injury is the leading cause of death in child maltreatment (e.g. acceleration-deceleration
forces (shaking), direct force application (blow or impact), or a combination of these forces)
+ consider “red herrings” (eg, slate grey macule/congenital dermal melanocytosis vs. bruises; cupping
practices that may look like bruising)

Investigations
+ document all injuries on a body diagram: type, location, size, shape, colour, pattern
= photography of skin injuries is ideal (police or hospital photography preferred; do not use physician's
personal camera)
rule out medical causes of bruising/fracture with appropriate investigations (e.g. blood disorders or rickets):
= if fractures evident: Ca**, Mg”, PO.*, ALP, Pin. itamin D, albumin
« if bruising present: CBC, INR, PTT, von Willebrand factor, factors VIII/IX
screen for abdominal trauma
= elevated transaminases and amylase or abdominal bruising: abdominal CT recommended
toxicology screen: overdose or poisonin;
skeletal survey in children <2 ye select imaging based on history in children >2 yr
= neuroimaging: CT and/or MRI - dilated eye examination by pediatric ophthalmologist to rule out
retinal hemorrhage if subdural hemorrhage detected on head imaging

Sexual Abuse

Definition

+ the involvement of a child or an adolescent in sexual activity that he or she does not fully comprehend and
is unable to give informed consent to, or for which the child or adolescent is not developmentally prepared
and cannot give consent [to], or that violates the laws or social taboos of society

History

. in prepubertal children, gather information regarding the sexual abuse from the caregiver in the absence of
the child

collect sufficient medical information and history to guide medical management, e.g. genital pain, bleeding
or discharge that may inform timing and need fora genital exam

Physical Exam
+ assess for head trauma, fractures
+ genital exam should be completed by a healthcare provider with training in the assessment of children/
youth who have been sexually abused/assaulted
= anogenital exam performed along with head-to-toe physical for physical trauma
= instrumentation not required for anogenital exam; speculum contraindicated in prepubertal girls
= most victims have normal anogenital exam - cannot rule out sexual abuse if exam is negative

Investigations
+ depend on presentation, age, sex, and pubertal development of child

= referral toa child maltreatment pediatrician or a sexual assault domestic violence centre for possible
sexual assault examination kit
rule out STI, pregnancy (consider STI prophylaxis or emergency contraception)
consider drug and alcohol screen (eg. Rol ypnol, Liquid Ge etc) if altered level of consciousness or
concerns for possible drug-facilitated sexual assault

Neglect

Definition
+ needs of the child are not met resulting in actual or potential harm

History
+ from child and each caregiver separately (if possible)
