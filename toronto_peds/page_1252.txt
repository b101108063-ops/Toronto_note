NON-HODGKIN LYMPHOMA

Epidemiology
+ incidence peaks at 7-11 yr

Clinical Features

+ presents as enlarging, non-tender Iymphadenopath

+ includes most commonly mature B cell lymphoma ‘Burkitt, diffuse large B cell), T cell lymphoblastic, and
mature T cell lymphoma (anaplastic large cell)

rapidly growing tumour with distant metastases (unlike adult non-Hodgkin lymphoma)

signs and symptoms related to disease site: most commonly abdomen (intussusception), chest (mediastinal
mass), head and neck region

Diagnosis
+ pathologic evaluation of affected tissue (e.g. abdominal mass, extranodal site, or lymph node)

Investigations
+ same investigations as Hodgkin lymphoma

Management
+ combination chemotherapy
+ noadded benefit of radiation in pediatric protocols

Prognosis
+ 75-90% 5 yr survival

Complications

+ mediastinal mass: compression of important structures (i.e. tracheal, SVC, ventricular outflow) causes
significant risk of cardiac or respiratory arrest during procedures where the patient is supine; instead,

perform these procedures with the patient on their side or prone

tumour lysis syndrome: results from rapid breakdown of malignant cells, causing metabolic abnormalities

including hyperuricemia, hyperkalemia, and hyperphosphatemia

Wilms’ Tumour (Nephroblastoma)

Epidemiology
+ usually diagnosed between 2-5 yr; M=F

+ most common primary renal neoplasm of childhood

+ In5-10% of cases, both kidneys are affected (simultaneously or in sequence)

Associated Congenital Abnormalities
+ WAGR syndrome (Wilms’ tumour, Aniridia, Genitourinary anomalies, Range of developmental delays)
with 11p13 deletion (~50% Wilms’ Tumour risk)
+ Beckwith-Wiedemann syndrome (5-10% Wilms’ Tumour risk):
= characterized by enlargement of body organs (especially tongue), hemihypertrophy, renal medullary
gps. adrenal cjtomegaly, omphalocele, ear creases, and macrosomia
= also at increased risk for developing hepatoblastoma, and less commonly adrenocortical tumours,
neuroblastomas, and rhabdomyosarcomas
+ Denys-Drash syndrome (90% Wilms’ Tumour risk): characterized by gonadal dysgenesis and nephropathy
leading to renal failure
