PNEUMONIA

+ see Respirology, P68

+ consider in infants with prolonged (218 h) or premature rupture of membranes, maternal fever or other
signs and symptoms of chorioamnionitis, or if mother is GBS positive

suspect if infant exhibits respiratory distress, temperature instability, or WBC is low (<5 x 10°/L), elevated
(>30 x 109/L), or left-shifted

symptoms may be non-specific (e.g. lethargy, apnea, tachycardia, poor perfusion, poor feeding)
investigations: CXR (hazy lung and/or distinct infiltrates, may be difficult to differentiate from RDS), chest
ultrasound (confirms pleural effusion in complicated pneumonia), blood and CSF cultures

* neonates with pneumonia should be admitted to the NICU and given empiric antibiotics for management

Investigations
+ suspicion of neonatal sepsis requires “full septic workup”
= Early onset sepsis: CBC, blood culture, LP (CSF analysis: cell count, glucose, protein, culture, and PCR
for viruses) + CXR
= LP must be conducted if blood culture is positive due to increased risk of meningitis; otherwise
controversial and depends on clinical presentation
= Add urine culture with late-onset sepsis

Management

+ supportive care

+ IV ampicillin + gentamicin (non-meningitic coverage) chosen as first-line empiric therapy for early onset
sepsis

if meningitis suspected or late onset sepsis, consider IV ampicillin + cefotaxime + vancomycin at

meningitic doses

choice of antibiotic and duration of treatment dependent on symptoms, culture results, maternal GBS

status, and local resistance patterns

addition of IV acyclovir if HSV infection suspected
