+ apply direct pressure on external wounds while elevating extremities. Do not remove impaled objects in the

«start two large bore (14-16 G) IVs in the brachial/cephalic vein of each arm or, if unavailable, obtain central

Glasgow Coma Scale

+ GCS is for use in trauma patients with decreased LOC; good indicator of severity of injury and
neurosurgical prognosis

+ most useful if repeated; change in GCS with time is more relevant than the absolute number

+ less meaningful for metabolic coma

* patient with deteriorating GCS needs immediate attention

+ prognosis based on best post-resuscitation GCS

+ reported as a 3-part score: Eyes + Verbal + Motor = Total

+ if patient intubated, GCS score reported out of 10 + T (T = tubed, i.e. no verbal component)

E, EXPOSURE/ENVIRONMENT

+ expose patient completely and assess entire body for injury; log roll to examine back

+ DRE for trauma patients at discretion of physician (e.g. pelvic fracture, abnormal neurological findings,
penetrating abdominal trauma)

+ keep patient warm (36°C) with a blanket + radiant heaters; avoid hypothermia

+ warm IV fluids/blood

+ keep providers safe (contamination, combative patient, environmental hazards)

2. Resuscitation

+ done concurrently with primary survey

+ attend to ABCs

+ manage life-threatening problems as they are identified

+ vital signs q5-15 min

+ ECG, BP, and O2 monitors

+ Foley catheter and NG tube if indicated

+ tests and investigations: CBC, electrolytes, BUN, Cr, glucose, amylase, INR/PTT, B-hCG, liver panel, VBG,
lactate, troponin, toxicology screen, group and screen
