Approach to Cardiac Arrest in the Hypothermic Patient

treatment focus: active core re-warming (preferably with extracorporeal membrane oxygenation, ECMO,
over cardiopulmonary bypass)

intubate if required, ventilate with warmed, humidified O2

do all procedures gently or may precipitate VFib

check pulse and rhythm for at least 1 min; may have profound bradycardia

if any pulse at all (even very slow) do NOT do CPR

if in VFib try to defibrillate up to maximum 3 shocks if core temperature <30°C

intubate if required, ventilate with warmed, humidified O2

medications (vasopressors, antidysrhythmics) may not be effective at low temperatures (controversial); may
try one dose

. fovus of treatment is re-warming

FROSTBITE

Classification
ice crystals form between cells

+ classified according to extent of initial lesion at day 0 after rapid rewarming
+ Grade depth for frostbite injuries of the extremities:
+ Grade 1
= absence of initial lesion
+ Grade 2
= initial lesion on distal phalanx
+ Grade 3
= initial lesion on intermediary and proximal phalanx
+ Grade 4
= initial lesion on carpal/tarsal
Management
+ treat for hypothermia: 02, IV fluids, maintenance of body warmth
* remove wet and constrictive clothin;
+ immerse in 37-39 °C agitated water for 10-30 min (very painful; administer adequate analgesia)
+ clean injured area and leave it open to air
+ consider aspiration/debridement of blisters (controversial)
+ debride skin
+ tetanus prophylaxis
+ consider antibiotics as frostbite injury has high-risk of infection
+ surgical intervention may be required to release restrictive eschars (compartment syndrome)
+ never allow a thawed area to re-chill/freeze
+ consider using tPA and/or iloprost for severe burns, in conjunction with specialist advice

Burns
«see Plastic Surgery, PLI8

Clinical Features/Physical Exam Findings
+ burn size

= rule of nines; does not include Ist degree (superficial) burns
+ burn depth

superficial (Ist degree): epidermis only (e.g. sunburn), painful and tender to palpation

superficial partial thickness (2nd degree): extends to epidermis and superficial dermis, blister
formation occurs, very painful
deep partial thickness (2nd degree): involves hair follicles, sebaceous glands; skin is blistered, exposed
dermis is white to yellow, absent sensation
full thickness (3rd degree): epidermis and all dermal layers; skin is pale, insensate, and charred or
leathe:
deep ith degree): involvement of fat, muscle, even bone

Management
+ remove noxious agent/stop burning process and consider appropriate PPE usage
establish airway if needed (indicated with burns >40% BSA or smoke inhalation injury or signs of
significant upper airway burns)
resuscitation for 2nd and 3rd degree burns (after initiation of 2 large bore IVs)
fluid boluses if unstable
once stable, use Parkland or other formula to guide replacement of insensate losses
= Parkland Formula: Ringer’s lactate 4 cc/kg/% BSA burned; give half in first 8 h, half in next 16 h;
maintenance fluids are also required if patient cannot tolerate PO hydration
= urine output is best measure of resuscitation, should be 40-50 cc/h or 0.5 cc/kg/h; avoid diuretics
+ pain relief: continuous opioid (e.g. hydromorphone, fentanyl, morphine) infusion for pain with breakthrough
loses PRN
