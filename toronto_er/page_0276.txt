Bradydysrhythmias and AV Conduction Blocks
+ AV conduction blocks
= Ist degree: prolonged PR interval (>200 mseo), no treatment required
= 2nd degree
* Mobitz I: gradual prolongation of PR interval then dropped QRS complex, usually benign
¢ Mobitz II: PR interval constant with dropped QRS complex, can progress to 3rd degree AV block
= 3rd degree: P wave unrelated to QRS complex, PP and RR intervals constant
* treatment: atropine and transcutaneous/transvenous pacing (atropine with caution) if unstable
+ if above fails, consider IV dopamine, epinephrine
= long-term treatment for Mobitz II and 3rd degree block: internal pacemaker
+ sinus bradycardia (rate <60 bpm)
= canbe normal (especially in athletes)
= causes: vagal stimulation, vomiting, MI/ischemia, increased ICP, sinus node dysfunction,
hypothyroidism, drugs (e.g. B-blockers, calcium channel blockers)
» look for up-right P waves in lead II and/or biphasic in lead V1 - confirms sinus P wave origin
= treat if symptomatic (hypotension, chest pain)
* acute: atropine + transcutaneous/transvenous pacing
sick sinus: transcutaneous/transvenous pacins
+ drug induced: discontinue/reduce offending drug, consider antidotes, pacing often ineffective, use
IV epinephrine to increase chronotropy

Supraventricular Tachydysrhythmias (narrow QRS)
+ sinus tachycardia (rate >100 bpm)
= causes: increased sympathetic tone, drugs, fever, shock, anemia, thyrotoxicosis, MI, PE, emotional,
ain, etc.
. Fear for and treat underlying cause
+ atrioventricular reentrant tachycardia (AVRT) / atrioventricular nodal reentrant tachycardia (AVNRT) (see
Cardiology and Cardiac Surgery, C24)
_ vagal maneuvers eg. carotid massage, Valsalva)
= continuous 12-lead ECG helpful during maneuvers or cardioversion if Ist episode; will help to elicit
underlying etiology
= adenosine 6 mg IV push, if no conversion give 12 mg, can repeat 12 mg dose once; warn patients
regarding side effects (e.g. chest pain, nausea)
