Afticville, Nova Scotia

+ Halifax was founded in 1749

+ African people, many of whom were descendants of slaves in Jamaica, dug out roads and built much of the
city

the early Black community lived a few kilometres north of the city in a community established on the
Bedford Basin in Halifax, an area that became Africville

the proximity to the waterfront for fishing, prospects for wage labour in the city, and establishment of
structures including a post office, school, and church created a tight-knit Black community which, at one
point, housed over 400 individuals and families

facilities deemed otherwise unfit for surrounding areas were established in Africville by city council and
businesses, including an oil plant, tar factory, prison, hospital for infectious diseases, and open garbage
dump, with raw sewage and waste products emptying directly into the water supply

the community was denied resources available to predominantly White neighbouring areas, despite
being within the municipal jurisdiction, including garbage collection, law enforcement, paved roads, and
appropriate water treatment

in 1957, the city expropriated the land for industrial use and forced the relocation of residents, many of
whom went into public housing

a settlement was reached between some former A fricville residents and the City of Halifax in 2010,
although an application for a class-action lawsuit submitted to the Supreme Court of Halifax was
overturned in 2018

on 24 February 2010, Halifax Regional Municipality Mayor Peter Kelly apologized for the destruction of
Africville

Climate Change and Health

climate change is a result of increased greenhouse gases from burning fossil fuels and destruction of

carbon sinks (e.g. forests) leading to global temperature increases, ocean acidification, and altered weather
atterns

the World Health Organization projects climate change to cause an estimated 250,000 additional deaths

annually by 2030

physicians should take into account how climate change may be affecting a patient's presenting complaint

climate change can lead to new climate-related hazards listed below, which can adversely affect a patient’s

health:

extreme weather events (floods, wildfires, landslides): injuries, deaths

reduced air quality: respiratory conditions, cardiovascular disease, allergies

altered distribution of vectors (e.g. mosquitos): increased incidence and spread of Lyme disease, West,

Nile virus, Malaria, Dengue

crop damage: malnutrition, food-borne illnesses

water supply damage: water-borne diseases, parasites, algal blooms

Public Health Approaches to the Climate Crisis

the public health response to climate change involves two approaches: mitigation to address the immediate
impacts, and adaptation to build long-term resilience

mitigation: efforts to slow, stabilize, or reverse climate change by directly reducing greenhouse gas
emissions

adaptation: anticipating and preparing for the effects of climate change (e.g. conduct health vulnerability
and adaptation assessments to identify at-risk populations and evaluate the resilience of current health
systems)
