Canada’s Low-Risk Alcohol Drinking Guidelines

Moderate Drinking

Men: 3 orless/d (<15/wk) Women: 2 or less (104k) Elderly: or essid

Biochemical Markers of Prolonged Alcohol Use
+ elevated liver function tests (AST, ALT, GGT), MCV, and carbohydrate-deficient transferrin (CDT)
+ AST:ALT ratio >2:1 and elevated GGT are suggestive of alcohol use

Alcohol Intoxication

+ throughout Canada, the legal limit for impaired driving is a BAC 20.08% (280 mg/dL or 17.4 mmol/L)
which is typically reached after 4 drinks in women and 5 drinks in men ina 2h period

most signs of intoxication are present at over >21.7 mmol/L (100 mg/dL): altered perception, impaired
judgement, ataxia, hyper-reflexia, impaired coordination, changes in mood/personality, prolonged reaction
time, and slurred speech

respiratory depression and arrest can occur with >60 mmol/L (non-tolerant drinkers) and 90-120 mmol/L
(tolerant drinkers)

Management of Alcohol Intoxication

+ stabilize patient if there is reduced level of consciousness or vomiting; assess airways and respiratory
function

administer IV crystalloid fluids if evidence of volume depletion or shock; correct electrolytes and

hypoglycemia
monitor for signs of alcohol withdrawal following detoxification in patients with alcohol use disorder
