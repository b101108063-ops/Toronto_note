Epidemiology

+ lifetime prevalence in Canada: 9%; F:M = 2:1

+ age of onset: mid-late 20s

+ risk factors: severity, duration, and proximity to trauma

* most common forms of trauma: unexpected death of someone close, sexual assault, serious illness or injury
to someone close, physical assault by partner or caregiver

+ 75% have another comorbid psychiatric disorder; increased risk of suicide 2-3x

+ DDx: bipolar disorder, borderline personality disorder, acute stress disorder (3 d-1 mo after trauma)

Treatment
+ 3stages of trauma recovery; treatment must be appropriate to patient’s stage
= stage 1 - safety and stabilization: emotional regulation techniques (i.e. breathing, relaxation) to help
build coping skills, medications for PTSD, manage substance use
= stage 2 - remembrance and mourning: exposure to traumatic memories and work through distorted
thoughts, relational patterns, and grief
= stage 3 - reconnection and integration: exposure therapy, etc. create a new future, new relationships,
strengthen identity
+ early intervention via psychological support (not de-briefing)
+ psychotherapy: CBT, DBT, supportive, eye movement desensitization and reprocessing (EMDR)
+ biological
. fst line: fluoxetine, paroxetine, sertraline, venlafaxine XR (50-80% response with residual symptoms
is common)
= prazosin (for treating disturbing dreams and nightmares)
= benzodiazepines (for acute anxiety; use with extreme caution, not recommended acutely post trauma)
= adjunctive atypical antipsychotics (risperidone, olanzapine)

Prognosis and Complications

+ prognosis: 50% of patients with PTSD have complete recovery within 3 mo, symptoms tend to diminish
with age

+ complications: substance use disorder, relationship difficulties, depression, impaired social and
occupational functioning disorders, personality disorders
