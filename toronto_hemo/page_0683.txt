H29 Hematology

Pathophysiology
+ primary or secondary ITP
+ an acquired immune-mediated disorder
= increased platelet destruction which may be primary or induced by medications, viral infections,
autoimmune diseases, or pregnancy
+ anti-platelet Abs bind to platelet surface > increased splenic clearance
+ helper T-cell and cytotoxic T-cell activation
+ impaired platelet production
= myelosuppression mediated by cytotoxic chemotherapy
= drug induced megakaryocyte suppression

Clinical Features

+ variable presentation: asymptomatic, fatigue, minimal bruising, mucocutaneous bleeding (e.g. purpura,
ecchymoses, petechiae, continuous epistaxis, menorrhagia) most common but bleeding can occur
anywhere including intracranial hemorrhage

+ assess for symptoms/signs suggesting a secondary cause

Investigations
+ CBC: platelet count <100x 10°/L
+ PTand PTT: normal
+ peripheral blood film: decreased platelets + large platelets (rule out platelet clumping)
+ HIV, HCV
+ depending on clinical suspicion: H. pylori testing (urea breath test, stool antigen, or endoscopy), vitamin
Biz, ANA, C3, C4, APLA, quantitative immunoglobulins (to rule out underlying immunodeficiency)
depending on clinical symptoms
blood group, RhD typing, direct antiglobulin test
BM aspirate and biopsy: increased number of megakaryocytes
= BM aspirate and biopsy should be considered pre-splenectomy to confirm diagnosis or if there is
suspicion of diminished BM function (systemic symptoms, refractory ITP, and/or abnormal blood film)

Treatment
+ rarely indicated if platelets >30 x 10°/L unless active bleeding, trauma, or surgery
* emergency treatment (active bleeding (CNS, GI, or GU) or in need of emergency surgery):

= general measures: stop antiplatelet medications, control blood pressure, minimize trauma
corticosteroids: prednisone (0.5-2 mg/kg/d) or dexamethasone (40 mg PO once daily x 4 d)
if corticosteroids contraindicated: IVIg 1 g/kg x 1 dose, to be repeated if necessary (raises platelet count
faster than corticosteroids)
IVIg can be used with corticosteroids when a more rapid increase in platelet count is required
antifibrinolytic: tranexamic acid (1 g PO TID or 1 g IV q8h) if mucosal bleeding
platelet transfusion: for refractory, major bleeding, or need for urgent surgery (expect that platelet
recovery will be diminished)
management of life threatening bleeding (i.e. intracranial bleeding): IV steroids, IVIg, platelet
transfusion, may also consider emergency splenectomy - vaccinations prior if possible (S. pneumoniae,
N. meningitidis, Meningococcus (quad, serotype b), Pneumococcus, and H. influenzae type b)
= management of intracranial bleeding: IV steroids, IVIg, platelet transfusion
+ non-urgent treatment (platelet count <20-30 x 10°/L and no bleeding):

= Ist-line:

+ corticosteroids (dexamethasone 40 mg PO once daily x 4 dx 1-4 cycles (not wk) or prednisone (0.5-2
mg/kg/d) x 2-3 wk then slow taper over 6 wk)

+ IVigl gikg

= 2nd-line:

+ splenectomy (need vaccinations prior to splenectomy: S. pneumoniae, N. meningitidis,
Meningococcus (quad, serotype b), Pneumococcus, and H. influenzae type b) - not preferred if
within 12 mo from diagnosis

+ thrombopoietin (TPO) receptor agonists (romiplostim, eltrombopag) - may not be accessible as
second line due to funding considerations

rituximab

= 3rd-line:

+ immunomodulating therapy (mycophenolate, azathioprine, cyclophosphamide, danazol, and
vincristine)

+ Syk inhibitors (Fostamatinib) - blocks platelet clearance

Definitions of Response to Treatment

+ complete response: platelet count >100 x 10°/L
+ partial response: platelet count 30-100 x 107/L
* no response: platelet count <30x 10°/L
