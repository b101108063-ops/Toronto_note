Umbilical Cord Prolapse

Definition
+ descent of the cord to a level adjacent to or below the presenting part, causing cord compression between
presenting part and pelvis

Etiology/Epidemiology

+ increased incidence with prematurity/PROM, fetal malpresentation (~50% of cases), low-lying placenta,
polyhydramnios, multiple gestation, and CPD

+ incidence: 0.16-0.18% of deliveries

Presentation
+ visible or palpable cord
+ FHR changes (variable decelerations, bradycardia, or both)

Treatment
+ emergency CD ifnot fully dilated and vaginal delivery not imminent
+ 02 to mother, monitor FHR
+ keep cord warm and moist by replacing it into the vagina + applying warm saline soaks
+ intrauterine resuscitation by alleviating pressure of the presenting part on the cord
= manually elevate fetal head with a pelvic exam
= place patient in steep Trendelenburg or knee-to-chest position
« retrofilling the bladder with 500-700 mL of normal saline
+ iffetal demise or too premature (<22 wk GA), allow labour and delivery

Uterine Rupture

Definition
+ complete separation of all layers of the uterine wall

Etiology

+ associated with previous uterine scar (in 40% of cases), tachysystole with oxytocin, grand multiparity, and
previous intrauterine manipulation

+ generally occurs during labour, but can occur earlier with a classical incision

+ 0,5-0.8% incidence, up to 12% with classical incision

Risk Factors

+ uterine scarring (e.g. previous uterine surgeries including CD (especially classical incision), perforation
with D&C, and myomectomy)

excessive uterine stimulation (e.g. protracted labour, oxytocin, and prostaglandins)

uterine trauma (e.g, operative equipment, ECV)

multiparity

uterine abnormalities

malpresentation

placenta accreta
