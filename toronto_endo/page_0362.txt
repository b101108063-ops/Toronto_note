Gynecomas'

Definition

true gynecomastia refers to benign proliferation of the glandular component of the male breast, resulting in
the formation of a concentric, rubbery, firm mass extending from the nipple(s)

physiologic gynecomastia - trimodal distribution in neonatal, pubertal, and older males
pseudogynecomastia or lipomastia refers to enlargement of soft adipose tissue, especially seen in obese
individuals

Etiology

physiologic gynecomastia - trimodal distribution in neonatal, pubertal, and older males

drugs such as spironolactone, cimetidine, digoxin, ketoconazole, recombinant human GH, hCG, estrogens,
antiandrogens, GnRH agonists, 5-a-reductase inhibitors, androgen deprivation therapy (ADT), anabolic/
doping misuse

surgical ADT (orchiectomy) for prostate cancer

starvation and refeeding

male hypogonadism

cirrhosis ~ due to reduced ability of liver to metabolize and inactivate estrogens

treatment of HIV infection - due to fat tissue as part of lipodystrophy

herbal products ~ plant-derived oils such as lavender and tea tree oil

idiopathic

testicular/germ-cell neoplasms

CKD

other rare causes: feminizing adrenal tumours, disorders of sex development, ectopic hCG, familial
prepubertal ynecomastia
+ hyperthyroidism
Pathophysiology
+ hormonal imbalance due to:
= increased estrogen activity
+ increased production, or increased availability of estrogen precursors for peripheral conversion to
estrogen, or decreased metabolism/inactivation
= decreased androgen activity
¢ decreased androgen production, binding of androgen to sex hormone binding globulin (SHBG), or
androgen receptor blockage

History

+ present for >12 mo or <12 mo

+ recent change in breast characteristics
nipple discharge

pain

trauma to testicles

mumps

+ alcohol and/or drug use

+ FHx

+ sexual dysfunction

Physical Exam
+ signs of feminization
+ breast
= rule out red flags suggesting breast cancer: unilateral, eccentric, hard or fixed mass, skin dimpling or
retraction, and nipple discharge (especially bloody) or crusting
= gynecomastia occurs concentrically around nipple and has rubbery feel, is not fixed to underlying
tissue
+ genito-urinary exam (presence of pubic hair, testes size and location)
+ stigmata of liver or thyroid disease

Investigations

+ laboratory: serum TSH, PRL, LH, FSH, total testosterone, estradiol, LFTs, creatinine, hCG (if hCG is
elevated, need to locate the primary tumour), + ferritin (if indicated); not all investigations are required for
every case of gynecomastia

+ if the differentiation between gynecomastia and breast carcinoma cannot be made on the basis of clinical
findings alone, the patient should undergo breast U/S and consideration of diagnostic mammography,
which has 90% sensitivity and specificity for distinguishing malignant from benign breast diseases

+ CXR and CT of chest/abdomen/pelvis (to locate neoplasm)
